In 1969 a small town library opened its basement archive to the public for the first time.

Inside were more than 3,000 letters written by factory workers between 1920 and 1950. Most had never been read by anyone outside the families who donated them.

A volunteer named Ayşe began sorting the letters by date. She noticed that dozens mentioned the same strike in the winter of 1934.

Newspapers from that year barely covered the strike. The letters describe it in detail: the closed gates, the shared meals, the slow return to work.

Today the collection has been digitised, and historians use it to reconstruct a story the official record left out.
